## One-Line Memory

One sentence that helps recover the story months later.

## What Happened

Capture the facts in plain language. Do not polish yet.

## The Moment

What exact moment, image, line, decision, conflict, failure, realization, or change makes this story worth remembering?

## The People

Who was involved, and what did each person want, believe, fear, misunderstand, or decide?

Use roles rather than names when privacy or audience appropriateness matters.

## The System

What larger system was operating around the people?

Consider:
- incentives;
- rules;
- physical constraints;
- process;
- information;
- hierarchy;
- feedback;
- environment;
- timing;
- tools;
- culture.

## Tension

What was not working?

Why was the obvious solution insufficient?

## Choice / Turning Point

What did someone choose to do differently?

If nobody made a meaningful choice, identify what changed in the system instead.

## Result

What happened afterward?

Use concrete results where available. Avoid embellishing causality.

## What This Story Proves

What platform idea does this story provide evidence for?

Complete this sentence:

> This story matters because...

## Transfer

How does the lesson travel beyond the original setting?

Examples:
- from rowing to management;
- from boat handling to organizational leadership;
- from surveying to process design;
- from aviation to decision-making.

## Possible Takeaway

A concise audience-facing thought, not necessarily final wording.

## Lines Worth Keeping

Preserve any natural phrase, joke, image, dialogue, or sentence that may be useful later.

## 30-Second Version

The shortest complete telling of the story.

## 2-Minute Version

A compact version with setup, tension, turn, and takeaway.

## Full Version

The longer form for keynote development.

## Evidence / Artifacts

Links or references to anything that can verify, enrich, or visually support the story:
- photos;
- video;
- results;
- documents;
- emails;
- data;
- maps;
- race results;
- project files.

## Notes for Use

Anything to avoid, anonymize, verify, or adapt depending on audience.
